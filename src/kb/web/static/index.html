Scenario

A payroll consultant searches for double holiday pay and finds three versions: an official 2025 policy, a copy pasted from Teams with a different percentage, and an old intranet FAQ. Which one should they trust?

The pipeline

  1. Access control. PostgreSQL row-level security only returns documents shared with your groups. Hidden documents never take a slot in the results. See What I can see.
  2. Filters (optional). Country, department, source, language, tag, in force on a date. Run in SQL before ranking.
  3. Relevance. Keyword = BM25 computed in SQL (shows matched terms). Meaning = multilingual embeddings in pgvector (Dutch queries find English documents). Hybrid fuses both rankings with reciprocal rank fusion.
  4. Rerank (optional). A cross-encoder (multilingual jina-reranker-v2) reads the question together with each of the top 20 candidates and re-scores them. More accurate than the first pass, too slow to run on everything, so it only sees the shortlist.
  5. Context ranking. Each result is scored against who you are and how trustworthy it is. Every adjustment is a sentence you can read: green reasons, amber warnings. Switch each signal off in the sidebar to see its effect.
  6. Where results disagree. Facts (percentages, euro amounts, numbers of days, weeks, months, years) are extracted from every result. Facts of the same kind, in sentences about the same thing, for the same country, are compared. Different values are shown side by side with the quoted sentences, the best-ranked source first with its reasons, the others with their warnings. Different countries are never compared: that is a different context, not a contradiction.
  7. Ask a person. When no result really answers the question, the best result cannot be relied on, or the sources disagree without a clear winner, the page recommends people instead. Experts live in a separate database and are ranked the same way as documents: keyword and meaning match on their expertise, rerank, then context signals (same country and department, owns a document in your results, owns the most trustworthy version, out of office, inactive, experience), each with a reason or warning.
  8. Duplicate detection on upload. Near-copies notify the uploader, the other uploader and group managers, without naming documents they cannot read.

Context signals and weights

SignalUpDown
country+30% your country−50% another country
department+20% your department−20% another department
location+10% your site
validity−60% expired, −30% not yet valid
freshness+20% updated within a year−30% older than 3 years
source+20% official policy−10% Teams / email / chat
owner−10% no accountable owner
uploader+10% uploaded by a manager of its group
conflicts−40% contradicts a more trustworthy result

Final score = relevance × (1 + sum of adjustments), never below 10% of relevance. With rerank on, relevance is the cross-encoder's score (0 to 100%).
